Scaling a regulated payments product

Building the research practice that turned a compliance roadblock into a scalable onboarding program for complex business customers.

The challenge

Booking.com was expanding its regulated payment services to cover complex business customer structures: large, multi-entity organisations that represent the majority of eligible transaction value in the market. Verification rates for existing business customers were critically low (about 2%), and there was no research framework or compliance approach designed for the complexity these customers brought.

I joined this product area as the first dedicated researcher, with no prior online travel benchmarks, low internal understanding of these users, and high friction reported by customers.

Why it was complex

The work sat at the intersection of regulatory requirements, financial product complexity and user experience: three areas that are rarely researched together. Stakeholders spanned three legal entities and six departments, each with different priorities. Recruiting the right customers (niche, high-value, or those who had already failed verification) needed creative approaches that previous teams hadn't managed to solve.

The research program

Phase 1 · Q1 2025

Laying the strategic foundation

Qualitative deep dive into customers' business and legal structures, triangulated with internal segmentation data to define the compliance and onboarding approach for complex customers.

Phase 2 · Q2 2025

Enabling product release

Rapid-cycle evaluative research aligned to pilot timelines. Critical verification failure points were found and mitigated before launch, supporting a confident go/no-go decision.

Phase 3 · H2 2025

Future-proofing the platform

Mixed-methods research with customers and connectivity providers to guide platform strategy and de-risk expansion to the next tier of customer complexity.

Methods

  • In-depth interviews
  • Usability testing (live product)
  • Rapid evaluative research
  • Internal data triangulation
  • Stakeholder workshops
  • Intercept recruitment
  • Survey research
  • Mixed methods

Approach highlights

One of the most consequential decisions was proactively expanding the initial research brief, before any fieldwork, to cover all compliance scenarios rather than the narrow set originally scoped. That meant pushing back on stakeholder assumptions and making the case for broader coverage. It proved critical: Phase 1 showed that the compliance framework in development didn't reflect how customers actually structured their businesses, and would have created major onboarding barriers at scale.

Instead of prototypes, I worked with Engineering to enable live testing of the onboarding flow before launch. This surfaced real verification failures in context so they could be mitigated ahead of the pilot. I also ran several recruitment streams at once to reach the hardest-to-reach participants: customers who had already failed verification, and high-value enterprise accounts.

Impact

  • Phase 1 findings influenced senior leadership to overhaul the compliance framework, replacing a complex scenario-based approach with a simplified, risk-based model and unblocking the majority of eligible transaction value for the program.
  • Customer verification success improved from ~2% to 23% following the pilot, with approximately 9,000 business customers onboarded.
  • Critical verification failures were identified and mitigated pre-launch, enabling a successful pilot that had previously been paused because verification rates were unacceptable.
  • Insights shaped the 2025–26 product roadmap, including prioritisation of four new initiatives and direction for the group-customer MVP (in build: 2026).
  • Transformed the relationship between Legal & Compliance and UX: Legal now proactively surfaces research needs and engages directly with the customer perspective.
  • Platform research steered the organisation away from a high-cost technical integration in favour of a more scalable, targeted approach.
